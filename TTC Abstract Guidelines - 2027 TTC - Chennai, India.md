# Guidelines on TTC Abstracts

## **Introduction**

The Trimble Technical Conference (TTC) is the premier event for showcasing our company's innovation and engineering excellence. The heart of the TTC is the technical presentations from you, the participants. These guidelines are designed to help you prepare a successful submission for the 2027 conference in Chennai, India.

To be considered as a speaker, at registration time, please provide a title and an abstract of what you would like to present. This abstract is the only information the reviewers have to decide whether your presentation will be accepted as oral or poster presentation. Submission and acceptance of your abstract guarantees your approval to attend the TTC as a speaker or poster presenter. Approval of your attendance by your management chain is still required.

## **Guidelines for Reviewers: Evaluating TTC Abstracts**

Evaluate each abstract on a scale of 1 (Poor) to 5 (Excellent) across the following three dimensions.

### 1\. Technical, Product & Strategic Excellence

Instructions: Evaluate the excellence and strategic value of the work. This criterion balances alignment with Trimble's future vision (Data/AI) with genuine innovation in any domain (Engineering, Hardware, Software, or Product Management).

* Strategic Alignment: Does it advance *Connect & Scale* (integrated workflows, APIs)? Read more about Connect & Scale: [Updated\_Trimble as a Platform\_January 2026\_Public](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1KngXrs91zcPXAbkbhy57TAsIsnQZcHMWMiY1Yx2-QgI/edit?usp=sharing).  
  Does it leverage AI/ML or treat Data as a Core Asset? *(Note: Lack of AI/Data focus can be offset by exceptional innovation in other areas).*  
* Innovation & Excellence: Does it utilize technologies in a unique way? Does it demonstrate exceptional approaches in Product Management (e.g., novel go-to-market strategies, innovative business models, deep customer discovery, or product-led growth)? Is this a best practice we *should* be sharing, or is it an anti-pattern?

Scoring Guidance:

* 1-2 (Poor): A siloed, standard implementation with no strategic alignment and limited innovation in engineering or product management.  
* 3 (Medium): Shows some strategic elements or solid execution, but lacks a holistic platform vision, advanced intelligence, or significant novelty in its approach.  
* 4-5 (Excellent): A prime example of excellence. It either strongly aligns with our AI/Data strategy OR demonstrates exceptional, shareable innovation in engineering, hardware, software, or product management practices.

  ### 2\. Value Creation & Audience Appeal

Instructions: Assess the tangible impact of the project and its value to the conference attendees. High scores can be achieved through *either* strong internal value *or* strong external customer value.

* Internal OR External Value: Does it create clear Customer Value (making work explicitly Better, Faster, Safer, Cheaper, or Greener)? OR Does it create clear Internal Value (a highly reusable tool, pattern, or product strategy that can be leveraged by other Trimble teams)?  
* Audience Appeal: Will the subject matter have broad appeal? Would an attendee walk away with actionable insights they didn't possess before?

Scoring Guidance:

* 1-2 (Poor): Highly specialized topic with no clear value (internal or external) and no clear takeaways for the audience.  
* 3 (Medium): The topic has potential interest and offers some value, but the takeaways are vague, or the benefit (to customers or other teams) is not clearly articulated.  
* 4-5 (Excellent): Clearly delivers significant value (either solving a major customer pain point OR providing a highly reusable internal tool/strategy) AND offers valuable lessons attendees can apply to their own work. Includes at leas 1-2 key takeaways for the audience.

  ### 3\. Abstract Clarity & Reality Check

Instructions: Evaluate the quality and structure of the abstract itself.

* Clarity: Does it clearly define the problem being solved? Does it detail the methods/solution, and present the results/impact in a logical flow?  
* Reality Check: Does the system, product, or strategy presented currently exist and have proven results, or is it just a theoretical idea?

Scoring Guidance:

* 1-2 (Poor): Disorganized; it is difficult to understand exactly what problem is being solved, how it was achieved, or if the solution actually works.  
* 3 (Medium): The abstract has a solid core idea, but the delivery is missing. The problem is implied but not explicitly stated, or it is missing key structural elements (like specific methods or results). KEY: *With some guidance, you believe the presentation would be worth attending.*  
* 4-5 (Excellent): Exceptionally clear; opens with a distinct problem statement, followed by a logical progression through a proven solution to the final impact.

Nathan Patton, Michael Peterson: Pokémon Go: The blueprint to mapping every construction site in the world

[Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1nNLVp7Bs-oY220AlTJDNJjMixDQ46Tu0/view?usp=sharing) | [Presentation](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1wFlqGwYCwshFiQfladvlH41SBERJaOfeVYFNARofokw/edit?usp=sharing)

**Abstract**

***Note**: This abstract is great for its **visionary use** of gamification and crowdsourcing to solve a **pervasive industry problem**, demonstrating significant **business value** and **strategic alignment with AI/Data**.*

In 2004, Google set out to map the world. As they layered satellite imagery, 3D models, and Street View data, they hit a fundamental problem—mapping the physical world is an ongoing challenge, constantly changing and impossible to capture with manual processes and limited resources. This problem is mirrored in the Construction industry. Job sites are living, breathing environments—constantly evolving, difficult to capture, and inherently chaotic. Traditional reality capture requires dedicated scanning workflows, which, while powerful, struggle with adoption due to time, cost, and friction in setup. What if we could crowdsource this data passively, leveraging every worker, every piece of equipment, and every tool as a reality capture device?

So how did Google solve their problem? They built Pokémon Go. 

Developed by the former founders of Google Earth, Pokemon Go gamified reality capture. Players, driven by the hunt for rare Pokémon, unknowingly helped stitch together an incredibly detailed 3D map of the world. By leveraging tens of millions of users passively capturing data through their smartphones, Niantic filled gaps in traditional mapping methods, feeding this data back to Google, enabling them to build a more robust, flexible, and updating map of the world. This sparked an idea within Trimble. In 2022, we began detailing Project Maya, an initiative to generate crowdsourced maps through low cost reality capture sensors, driving mass data capture to enable automatic localisation, progress tracking, scheduling, and more. 

This session will detail how the playbook from Google and Pokemon Go is being repurposed within Trimble \- discussing how crowdsourced data from 360 cameras to iPhones to laser scanners can be used to create the most complete project management platform.

Key Takeaways for the Audience:  
\- How Google & Niantic transformed mapping through crowdsourcing and gamification  
\- The critical role of passive reality capture in solving construction’s biggest inefficiencies  
\- How Project Maya is building the most contextual map in the construction industry  
\- The potential for AI-driven workflows that automate progress tracking, payments, and decision-making.  
\- How we can use data to power workflows across Trimble products, including estimating, purchasing, project management, ERP, and more."

### **Level of Novelty/Innovation**

Joao Galinho \- Unlocking Fast and Accurate Map Updates through Optimization-driven Conflation Algorithm

[Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1UFV-Hya12XbvgdTB8ZWfJShfduvNNXY3/view?usp=sharing) | [Presentation](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1i4BTNFqOlhI6_YY6gCGFAKGQdFSI66Lqjq9XslpcO00/edit?usp=sharing)

**Abstract**

**Note**: This abstract stands out for its patent-pending, **novel** optimization **algorithm** that dramatically **boosts efficiency** and **accuracy** in core **data management**, unlocking significant **internal value** and potential for **scaling**.

Keeping our map data up to date is foundational to the value and success of all our products and solutions. However, there are over 50 million roads in the continental United States, and keeping all this map data up to date is a significant challenge. We have access to government sources that carry the newest map updates, but it is impossible to manually compare and validate each and every road.  
The Conflation algorithm is the key to semi-automate this process – the algorithm’s accuracy directly affects the speed, efficiency and accuracy at which our GIS team can incorporate changes into our map data. It matches each road in the government source to one or multiple roads in our map data, leaving the unmatched roads flagged as potential candidates for addition. The results are then manually reviewed by our GIS team to edit and add roads and attributes to our map data accordingly. Reducing the time spent by the GIS team to review Conflation results has long been a priority, so as to enable them to repurpose their time and efforts towards other high-value initiatives.  
As a foundational piece of intellectual property in the transportation business, our novel optimization-driven Conflation algorithm, which is already in production, improves upon the current academic state of the art for our real-world use cases and is currently patent pending. Compared to our previous algorithm, it reduces the time spent on Conflation by 29.6% and achieves a 10x decrease in the number of roads classified as conflicts – meaning the roads that the algorithm is unsure about.  
By framing Conflation as a linear programming optimization problem, the algorithm now considers the entire context of an area for each road it classifies and can natively handle many-to-one matches. This gives it a distinct advantage when dealing with more intricate scenarios, such as complex intersections and heavily gridded road networks, as well as makes it much more resilient to alignment issues between our map data and the target. Furthermore, this algorithm also introduces novel line geometry comparison methods and measurements – particularly a precise and computationally efficient mathematical adaptation of the Hausdorff distance to line geometries, as well as directed comparisons of line geometries according to their angle and overlap.  
The increased accuracy of our novel algorithm also opens the door to fully-automated use cases aimed at scaling our transportation business by streamlining integration with third-party map data. For scaling our transportation business to new markets in Europe – this algorithm could allow for a more accurate and efficient integration of our proprietary truck attribution and truck specific paths with partner’s maps. It can also contribute to easier connection and integration of map related data from current and future internal transportation partners.

Emelie Fogelqvist, Christian Graesser \- Dual-Camera Tracker for Quick Target Discovery and False-Target-Awareness

[Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Hre230N6RhLDtbWG19LB9N-Xmi-BTtEe/view?usp=sharing) | [Presentation](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Lln-owoykTpn_WgzFwOumduln8bl9V99U6goiEZMgm8/edit?usp=sharing)

**Abstract**

***Note:** This abstract is exemplary for its **innovative engineering solution** involving a dual-camera system to directly address a critical **customer pain point**, improving surveyor **productivity** and **reliability**.*

Background  
Modern-day surveyors are accustomed to automated workflows when using total stations. One example of such an automated process is identification and aiming to targets using prisms or active tracker targets. Trimble’s main interest for target tracking is clearly in total stations; however, the technology is also applicable inside scanners or autonomous vehicles to find reflective objects or targets for navigation. 

Problem  
A challenge for accurately tracking a target today is that false targets such as reflected light from the sun or moving car headlights are not sufficiently suppressed. This makes the tracker easily distracted as it may lock on to a false target instead of the desired target, which is both time-consuming and frustrating for the customer. The narrow field-of view required for high accuracy-tracking at long range limits the productivity at close-range, as the target may quickly move out of sight for the tracker and searching for targets is slower than it needs to be.

Solution  
The next generation total station that we are currently building comprises a dual-tracker utilizing a novel camera sensor with on-chip-subtraction and a narrow band-pass filter. It also contains the first camera-based tracker with the he capability to perform active tracking. The goal is to quickly find and never lose lock on the target.

Dual Tracker \- In addition to a typical tracker with a narrow field-of-view, the next generation total station contains an additional tracker with a significantly larger field of view. This allows us to optimize the field-of-view based on the target distance as well as considerably speeding up the search process. The wide-angle tracker also provides the possibility to monitor surrounding targets that are about to move into the narrow-angle tracker field-of-view. Improved algorithms will alert the tracker if a false target is detected, essentially eliminating the risk of losing lock to the desired target. The narrow-angle tracker is used for long range and high-precision tracking and is co-operating with the wide-angle tracker seamlessly, thereby saving time without increasing complexity for the customer.

Transmitter and Receiver Upgrades \- The technological improvements on the receiver side efficiently suppresses background noise and false targets by significantly reducing the time for differential-image capture. Another great advantage with the new tracker design is on the tracker transmitter side, where a VCSEL-array allows for higher laser power due to an extended light source. This is in addition to the VCSEL having a temperature-stable narrow-band spectrum, allowing for wavelength selection on the receiver side without losing signal.

Active tracking \- Using a target that emits light at a specific time ensures that the instrument locks on to the correct target; a key feature as reliability is part of the Trimble brand. The technological challenge here is synchronizing the remote active target (e.g. 800 m away) with the camera exposure time in the instrument.

Proven and Preliminary Results  
\- Field-of-view optimization improves search speed  
\- Background and false targets are suppressed with upgraded transmitter and receiver  
\- Tracker range is improved  
\- The instrument can synchronize with remote active targets

William Lentz, Fabian Rothmaier \- Addressing GNSS Jamming and Spoofing Threats

[Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1V8R6Jcgn5ZQS5e71zQoCOYwaWDvEEs5J/view?usp=sharing) | [Presentation](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1mMaJ0GNERQ8g5pgD4RqZ68A16N390kr-Ob32WZpNh8s/edit?usp=sharing)

**Abstract**

***Note:** This abstract is superb for tackling a **globally critical and evolving threat** to core **revenue** streams with cutting-edge mitigation strategies, showcasing vital **strategic relevance** and **exceptional engineering innovation.***

Trimble’s customers choose our GNSS products because they enhance productivity. These products enable faster, safer work in more locations compared to traditional methods. Increasingly, our customers are encountering significant jamming (loss of GNSS signals \- denial of service) and spoofing (fake GNSS signals \- misleading and hazardous information). Trimble’s GNSS portfolio captures physical data, plays a vital role in Trimble’s Connected Workflow, and generates several hundred million dollars annually with a high gross margin. This revenue is at risk if we fail to stay competitive. In recent years, jamming and spoofing have transitioned from rare threats to common occurrences in certain locations. These issues can reduce positioning accuracy or provide incorrect information, negatively impacting customer productivity. As GNSS systems are widely adopted, efficient backup systems are seldom found in our customers' workflows.

Over the past year, for the first time, we’ve observed spoofed positions “in the wild""; previously, this was limited to lab testing. In September 2024, an aviation working group published a report showing in excess of 1,000 flights impacted per day. Jamming has been a problem for many years but is usually localized and from low power jammers or faulty equipment. With the increase in conflicts has come an increase in GNSS jamming and spoofing, in large part to counter drones. Jamming and spoofing do not obey national borders; we’ve observed impact 100s of kilometers from conflict zones. While construction and geospatial users are not the target of the attacks, their operations are often collaterally damaged. 

This presentation will start with examples of real-world jamming and spoofing events experienced by our customers. At the last TTC, we presented Maxwell 8, our new baseband DSP ASIC. We now have that device, and this presentation will provide a brief update on the improved customer benefit it provides. However, customers have a significant number of units in the field with an older architecture. We’ll focus on innovative mitigation strategies that will be implemented in existing systems throughout 2025\. These mitigations cover enhancements to our:  
\- signal tracking (Stinger) and signal/measurement integrity,  
\- navigation message authentication (NMA),  
\- and precise positioning algorithms (ProPoint)

With these enhancements, if one or more GNSS bands are jammed or spoofed, we’ll continue to track and optimally position using the remaining unaffected bands. This limits the receiver’s ability to determine the ionospheric delay, often the dominant error in GNSS measurements. A particular problem as we are around the peak of the 11-year solar cycle. We’ll discuss how this affects the performance of the precision positioning solution and conclude with the results of our adapted ProPoint engine during various jamming and spoofing events, recorded both near conflict zones and during the 2024 “Jammertest” event in Norway. We will present how the implemented enhancements already provide our customers with seamless, highly accurate navigation in many scenarios and discuss the remaining challenges.
